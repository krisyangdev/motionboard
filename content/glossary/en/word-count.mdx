## How a word count works

A tool reads the text and splits it into words. For English, the simplest rule is that a word is a run of letters and digits between spaces or punctuation. Browsers can do this for many languages through a standard word segmentation feature, which also handles Chinese and Japanese, where words are not separated by spaces.

## Why two tools give different totals

The rules are not universal. Some programs count a hyphenated term like plug-in as one word, others split it in two. Numbers, slashes, dashes and abbreviations get their own treatment. The difference is usually a few words in a thousand, which matters only when a limit is tight.

## Words, characters and reading time

A word count is often shown next to the character count, with and without spaces. Platforms with a hard cap usually count characters, while essays and articles use words. Reading time is an estimate made by dividing the word count by an average speed, such as 238 words per minute for adults reading silently.

## Common confusions

A word count is not a page count: the same words fill more or fewer pages depending on font and spacing. It is also not a measure of quality. When a limit matters, count in the tool that will judge the text and leave a small margin.
